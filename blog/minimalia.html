Minimalia: A Minimalist Web Browser

A small, simple web browser I built in Python with PyQtWebEngine, because I wanted browsing to feel quiet again.

Coding Python App Development Minimalism

Minimalia is a desktop web browser I made in Python. I built it because I wanted browsing to feel less cluttered and less distracting. It uses the Chromium engine through PyQtWebEngine, so websites show up exactly the way they do in the big browsers. What's different is everything around the page, which I kept as small as I could.

I wanted to see how much of the usual browser interface I could take away before it started to feel like something was missing. It has a dark theme, easy-to-read text and not too much colour, and the navigation works the way you'd expect. That way your attention stays on the website you're actually looking at.

Why I Made It

Most browsers these days have packed toolbars, side panels, recommendations and a bunch of features that a lot of people hardly ever touch. I wanted something that felt light and simple, but was still familiar enough that you wouldn't have to learn anything new to use it.

What I Focused On

While I was designing Minimalia, I kept coming back to a few things:

  • As few buttons and bars as possible
  • Making it obvious what matters on the screen
  • Navigation that works the way you'd expect
  • Fewer things pulling at your attention
  • A dark, modern look that doesn't shout at you

I tried to get the same idea across in the branding and on the landing page. The tagline sums it up: “No clutter, no noise. Just the web, minimal and clean.”

What I Did

I made the whole thing myself. I wrote the app in Python, hooked up the browser engine, and came up with the idea, the interface and the overall look. I wanted it to be a more focused option for people who are tired of browsers that try to do everything. So I worked on pretty much every part of it, from how the tabs work and the settings screens to the branding and the landing page.

How It Works

Minimalia is a Python app built on PyQtWebEngine. That's the Qt binding that puts Chromium (the same engine behind Chrome and Edge) inside a normal desktop window. Splitting the work up like this is really what makes the project possible:

  • Chromium via QtWebEngine handles the actual web pages, so sites look and work the way they're supposed to.
  • Python and Qt handle everything around the page (tabs, the address bar, settings, bookmarks and downloads). That's where all the minimalist design happens.
  • Since the app around the page is mine, I can cut out clutter, add privacy features and plug the ad blocker straight into the engine.

Using Chromium does come with some limits, though. A couple of the things version 1.0.0 can't do yet (like Google sign-in) are down to QtWebEngine's policies rather than anything in Minimalia itself.

What's in Version 1.0.0

Version 1.0.0 is the first public release. It came out on 28 April 2026 for Windows, macOS and Linux, and even though it looks simple, there's quite a lot packed in.

Browsing

  • A tabbed interface with close, reopen, and keyboard shortcuts
  • Pinned tabs that survive across sessions, with accidental-close protection
  • Duplicate a tab instantly (Ctrl+Shift+D) and full session restore on reopen
  • Private browsing in an isolated profile that doesn't keep any history or cookies
  • Your choice of search engine: Google, DuckDuckGo, Bing, or a custom URL
  • Three experience modes: Minimalia, Cleanups, and Default

Privacy & Security

  • Built-in ad blocking powered by Brave's engine with EasyList filters
  • Promotion blocking on Bing and DuckDuckGo, and AI clutter stripped from major search engines
  • HTTPS-only mode with automatic protocol upgrades and WebRTC IP-leak protection
  • Explicit permission prompts for sensitive APIs, plus cookie and search-history controls

Navigation & Tools

  • Find-in-page (Ctrl+F), per-tab zoom, and developer tools (F12)
  • Print to PDF (Ctrl+P) with a save-location picker
  • Open links in a new tab (Ctrl+click), with favicons and security indicators on every tab

Bookmarks, History & Downloads

  • A bookmarks panel with right-click toggling and bulk deletion
  • A searchable history panel you can manage or clear
  • A download dialog with live progress, plus "Save Image As" with a folder picker

Setup

  • A first-run setup wizard and a unified settings screen
  • A profile-reset option and a built-in update notifier with snooze

There are a couple of things that don't work properly yet. Google sign-in is blocked right now because of Google's QtWebEngine policy, and Bing support is still experimental.

What I Was Going For

I didn't want to keep piling on features. I wanted to work out which ones actually make browsing better, and then get rid of the rest.
