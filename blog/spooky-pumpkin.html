Spooky Pumpkin: My Entry for the Playto Labs Robo Halloween Challenge 2026

A paper pumpkin that notices you walking past and laughs at you.

Robotics Electronics Python Pygame Halloween

For the Playto Labs Robo Halloween Challenge 2026, I wanted to make something spooky that actually reacts to you. So I built a pumpkin that waits for someone to walk past and then laughs at them.

It's called Spooky Pumpkin. There's an infrared (IR) sensor and some green LEDs on the electronics side, and the sound comes from a Python program.

The Idea

The finished Spooky Pumpkin, with the IR sensor tucked just under its chin.

Most Halloween decorations just sit there. I wanted one that would notice people.

The plan was pretty simple. Someone walks by, the pumpkin's eyes glow green, and a creepy cackle plays. Hopefully they jump a little.

How It Works

Here's what happens, and which part handles each step.

  1. Someone walks past
  2. IR sensor triggers
  3. Eyes glow green
  4. Cackling laugh plays
  1. IR Sensor: Picks up anyone passing in front of the pumpkin.
  2. Green LEDs: There are two of them, and they're the eyes. They switch on when the sensor is triggered, which gives the pumpkin a nice eerie glow.
  3. Python and PySerial: The sensor readings reach my Python program over serial communication. PySerial is the library that reads them.
  4. Pygame: Once Python knows someone is there, Pygame plays the cackling sound.

From the outside you don't see any of that. It just looks like the pumpkin woke up and spotted you.

Combining Hardware and Software

Inside the pumpkin: the Arduino Uno, breadboard and the two green LEDs that become its eyes.

The part I liked most was getting real electronics and my Python code to talk to each other.

The IR sensor is the input and the LEDs are what you see. The sound is all done in Python with PySerial and Pygame.

Without that connection it would just be a paper pumpkin with some lights in it.

What I Learned

Stuff I picked up along the way:

  • How an IR sensor can tell when a person or object is nearby.
  • Switching LEDs on based on what a sensor reads.
  • Using PySerial to get data from the hardware into Python.
  • Playing sound effects with Pygame.
  • Making hardware and software work together on one build.

Wrapping Up

This was a really fun one to make. I got to mess around with electronics and code, and it was a good excuse to get into the Halloween mood.

Now I've got a pumpkin with glowing green eyes and a creepy laugh, which is pretty much exactly what I was going for.

I'm happy with how it turned out as my entry for the Playto Labs Robo Halloween Challenge 2026.
